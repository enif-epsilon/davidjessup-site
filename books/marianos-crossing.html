Mariano's Crossing

A historical novel of early Colorado

Book One

Mariano Medina, a former mountain man and friend of Kit Carson, his wife Takánsy, and a young sawmill owner's son named John Alexander are drawn into a fight over the future of the Medinas' fifteen-year-old daughter, Lena. As old secrets surface, their struggle spirals toward a final, haunting reckoning.

The novel is grounded in real history: Medina was a significant figure in post–gold rush Colorado, best remembered for guiding a U.S. Army brigade through a brutal 1857 winter crossing.
